{# The thirty seconds. Generating a paper takes about half a minute, and until now the page did nothing while it happened: the button went dead, the tab spun, and a commissioner who has never used the site before had no way to tell a paper being written from a site that had hung. People in that position either leave or press the button again — and a second press can spend a regeneration. This covers the page the moment the form is submitted and stays until the server's answer arrives, at which point the browser navigates away and the overlay goes with the page. No polling, no background job: the POST is still an ordinary POST, which means every existing error path (out of regenerations, presses busy, an edited paper) still lands exactly where it did. The overlay only ever covers a request that is already in flight. Attach it to a form with data-generating. #}
{# A football in a spiral: rotating about its LONG axis, which is what a spiral actually is. Faked in 2D by sliding the laces across the ball and squashing them at the edges, so they appear to wrap round and disappear behind it. A flat 2D rotation would read as a ball tumbling end over end — a wobbly duck, which is the opposite joke. #}
