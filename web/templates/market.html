    What a player costs · staff only

    Every player starts where the country drafted him — {{ '{:,}'.format(R.picks) }} picks across {{ '{:,}'.format(R.drafts) }} leagues — and {{ '{:,}'.format(R.used) }} trades move him from there. A price is his place in the market, read as a draft pick: “costs the 2.03” means he is the fifteenth most expensive player, so the most expensive costs the first traded player off the board costs the earliest pick any of them went at. Up means he has passed players drafted ahead of him. A player with no trades has no market price and is listed separately. {{ R.rated }} players priced{% if R.streamers %}, {{ R.streamers }} kickers and defences left off{% endif %}.
    This is not a ranking of who is good. It is the going rate, and a player nobody will part with can sit cheap.

    How it works: a trade is read as a statement that two bundles are worth about the same, and each player moves by his own uncertainty divided by everyone’s — a player we barely know swings, one with forty trades behind him barely budges. Certainty grows with trades and decays without them, so a player nobody has touched since August becomes movable again rather than frozen at a preseason price. Firmness is how much of the draft’s original doubt the trades have removed.
    The curve from pick to value is fitted to the trades themselves (decay {{ R.decay }}, shift {{ R.shift }}), so the drafts supply the order and the trades supply the steepness. A roster spot is priced at {{ R.spot }}, because the side taking back fewer bodies is buying one.
    Trades carrying picks or FAAB are left out, and so are three-way deals. Dynasty leagues are not drafted against: a startup prices a career, not a season.

    Usage ranks a player by how many of the week’s {{ '{:,}'.format(a.teams) if a and a.teams else 'thousands of' }} teams start him and what he gives them when they do, measured against the last man anybody deliberately starts at his position. Book is then what the field pays a player of that standing, and the price is what it actually paid for him. So “22% over book” means the field pays him 22% more than it pays the typical player his usage looks like. Usage is a record and the price is a forecast, which is why the gap is the interesting part: it is the country changing its mind. A kicker is the clearest case — started by everybody, traded for by nobody, so he prices far under book.
    Trades carrying draft picks or FAAB are left out: nobody knows what the pick is worth, and guessing would corrupt the players beside it. Three-way deals are out too. Valuing picks is the next piece of work, and in a dynasty-heavy population it is the big one.
    A player shown as <1 is at the floor: trades fix the gaps BETWEEN players, never the bottom of the scale, so the cheapest come out pinned together rather than separated.
