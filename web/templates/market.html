    How the price is set

    Every player starts where the country drafted him ({{ '{:,}'.format(R.picks or 0) }} picks across {{ '{:,}'.format(R.drafts or 0) }} leagues) and {{ '{:,}'.format(R.used or 0) }} trades move him from there. A trade is read as two bundles worth about the same, and each player moves by his own uncertainty: a player we barely know swings, one with forty trades behind him barely budges. Certainty grows with trades and fades without them.

    Prices are places in the market — #1 is the most expensive player — so the scale can’t sag: somebody is always first. {{ R.rated }} players priced{% if R.streamers %}; {{ R.streamers }} kickers and defences left off, because they are streamed rather than traded for{% endif %}. Trades with draft picks or FAAB in them are left out, and so are three-way deals.

    This is not a ranking of who is good. It is the going rate, and a player nobody will part with doesn’t get one.
